The claim sounds like a feature: *the full NIFTY and BANKNIFTY option chain,
every market minute*. Every strike, CE and PE, with OI, IV and Greeks attached,
plus index futures, index spot and India VIX, in the same sixty-second window.

It is not a feature. It is a measurement with a timestamp on it — 26 August
2026 — and it started as a suspicion that the claim was impossible.

## The assumption that was backwards

Going in, full-chain-per-minute looked like a streaming problem. A WebSocket
connection, a careful enumeration of strikes, a rate budget with no slack in
it.

Reading the Fyers v3 API surface before writing code reversed all three:

- **REST `/data/options-chain-v3` returns the entire per-minute snapshot per
  index in about two calls** — per-leg OI, OI change, IV, Greeks, LTP, bid/ask
  and volume, plus aggregate call/put OI and the expiry list.
- **The WebSocket carries no OI, IV or Greeks.** The transport that looked
  mandatory is the one that cannot serve the fields that matter. It stays
  pluggable behind a `data_provider` interface, and is unused.
- **`strikecount` is the whole chain-scope story.** The endpoint returns
  strikes around ATM, so there is no strike enumeration to write.

## What the live spike said

Ran against the live market, one afternoon:

| Measurement | Result |
|---|---|
| Full cycle | 12 calls in **3.46 s**, against a 60 s budget |
| Rate budget | 12 calls/min against a 200/min cap — **~16× headroom** |
| Chain scope | ~202 legs per index per expiry at the configured `strikecount` |
| ATM legs | real OI, IV and Greeks — not placeholders |

Feasible, with room to spare. That verdict is why the collector exists; before
it, the whole idea was speculative.

## Production is a different problem

The spike proved one minute. Production had to answer a different question:
what happens on the 4,000th minute, when nobody is watching.

Four decisions carry most of the weight:

**A strict minute grid.** Snapshots land on `:00`, `:01`, `:02` — never
"roughly once a minute". A minute that cannot be captured in time is logged as
missing and skipped. That is the difference between a gap you can see and a
timestamp you cannot trust.

**One transaction per minute.** Each snapshot is a single SQLite transaction,
so a partial minute rolls back. No reader ever sees a chain with half its legs.

**Failing loud about the token.** Fyers access tokens are daily and refresh
tokens are not reliably available. Rather than store a PIN, the design accepts
one ~30-second login each morning through a local callback — and if the token
expires mid-session, the scheduler **stops and asks** rather than spinning on a
dead credential.

**Deriving nothing per minute.** IV and Greeks arrive attached. PCR and max-OI
are computed once at EOD. Capture raw; derive separately.

## Then Kubernetes

On 8 September the collector moved from cron-over-WSL with a watchdog to
**Kubernetes on Docker Desktop via Helm**: two always-on containers — the
collector and the token OAuth callback service — over one shared persistent
volume.

What the move actually bought was an answer to "what restarts it". A supervised
container with a persistent volume beats a cron entry and a PID file, and the
morning login became a one-click Telegram prompt at 06:30 instead of a terminal
you had to remember to open.

## What it costs, and what it weighs

A full snapshot is **11 option-chain calls plus 1 batched quotes call, about
2.5 seconds** — 12 calls a minute, a fraction of the cap. Roughly **2,070
option-chain rows and 7 quote rows per minute**, compressing to about **4.5 MB
a day**, roughly a gigabyte a year, archived as xz one day at a time.

That ratio — an entire index option chain a minute, for the price of a photo a
day — is the reason this is the foundation layer of everything else AlphaLens
intends to build. A backtester over a feed this complete is a different
proposition from a backtester over daily closes. The backtester does not exist
yet. The feed does.
