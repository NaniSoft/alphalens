<Note status="Research direction">No validation harness exists in AlphaLens,
and this site publishes no validation results.</Note>

Validation is the gate between "a strategy that backtested well" and "a strategy
worth risking money on". It is also the direction most often faked — a
single in-sample equity curve presented as evidence.

## What honest validation has to survive

- **Out-of-sample data the strategy never touched.** Requires archive depth the
  platform does not have yet.
- **Regime coverage** — bull, bear, high- and low-volatility stretches, not one
  convenient window.
- **Cost and capacity realism**, which for options means modelling fills the
  platform does not model.
- **A benchmark that means something** — the framework's `.NS → ^NSEI` mapping
  is the right default, and the [Reflector](/docs/research-pipeline/anatomy)
  design already computes realized versus alpha return against it.
- **Enough independent trials to reason about how many candidates were
  tried and discarded.** The number nobody publishes is the number that
  matters.

## Where the platform genuinely helps

The Reflector design is the useful part here, and it is a design: outcomes are
written to a memory log keyed against the benchmark, so a future run reads what
happened last time rather than re-deriving it. Combined with a
[feed](/docs/data-contract/data-feed-view) whose timestamp and symbol form are
canonical, that makes "the same experiment, re-run" a reproducible operation
instead of an aspiration.

## What it does not help

Nothing in AlphaLens decides whether a strategy is good. There is no scoring,
no ranking, no recommendation, and there will not be one described on this site
until there is one running — at which point the honest framing is still
*research*, not *advice*.
